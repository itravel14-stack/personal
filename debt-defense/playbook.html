CO

Colorado, specifically

Superior straddles the county line — most of the town is Boulder County (20th Judicial District, Justice Center at 1777 6th St, Boulder), a slice is Jefferson County (Golden). The caption on your summons says which.

Which court decides everything

This is good news for the arbitration play

Colorado’s small claims division bars attorney representation unless both sides have counsel, so collection firms file in the County Court civil division instead. County Court civil is not “small claims court” — so the small-claims carve-out that kills this strategy in other states probably does not reach your case.

Still read the exact words. Some clauses carve out “courts of limited jurisdiction” or any case under a dollar threshold rather than saying “small claims.” Colorado County Court is a court of limited jurisdiction. That single sentence in the contract is the whole ballgame.

Your deadline is a date, not a countdown

Do this first

County Court runs on the APPEARANCE DATE printed on your summons — C.R.C.P. 312. Not 21 days. Not 30. The date on the paper.

The 21-day figure everyone quotes online is C.R.C.P. 12(a), which governs District Court and probably doesn’t apply to you. The appearance date can be set up to 63 days from issuance, and you must have been served at least 14 days before it — so if you were served late in that window, your real deadline may be about two weeks out.

Find the date. Calendar it. Set the alarm five days early.

The form is free: CRCCP Form 3, “Answer Under Simplified Civil Procedure,” with instructions in JDF 110, at coloradojudicial.gov → Self-Help → Cases for $25,000 or Less. There’s a filing fee; ask the clerk about a JDF 205 fee waiver if that’s a problem.

Statute of limitations: six years, not three

C.R.S. § 13-80-103.5(1)(a) — six years for a “liquidated debt or an unliquidated, determinable amount of money.” Colorado courts treat credit card debt as liquidated. The “three years for contracts” figure circulating online is § 13-80-101 and does not govern card debt here. The clock runs from default, not from charge-off.

“No acknowledgment or promise shall be evidence of a new or continuing contract sufficient to take a case out of the operation of the statute of limitations, unless it is in writing signed by the party to be charged; but this section shall not alter the effect of a payment of principal or interest.”

C.R.S. § 13-80-113
Read that clause twice

In Colorado, a verbal “yeah, that’s mine” doesn’t revive a dead debt — a payment can. Case law softens it (the payment must be voluntary, made and accepted, and accompanied by an unqualified acknowledgment that more is owed), but don’t test the edges. Do not pay anything on an account near or past six years old.

Colorado’s best local weapon: the license

This defense doesn’t exist in most states, and it’s the first thing to check.

  • C.R.S. § 5-16-118 — anyone acting as a collection agency in Colorado must hold a valid license from the Administrator: the Colorado Attorney General’s Consumer Credit Unit / Collection Agency Board.
  • C.R.S. § 5-16-103 expressly defines debt buyers as collection agencies. Midland, Cavalry SPV, Velocity Investments — all of them need a Colorado license and a $12,000–$20,000 surety bond.
  • Exempt: attorneys (still bound by the CFDCPA’s substantive provisions) and original creditors collecting their own debts in their own name.

Look up the exact entity named as plaintiff on your summons at coag.gov → Consumer Protection → Consumer Credit Unit → Collection Agency Regulation. Not the parent company. Midland Funding LLC and Midland Credit Management, Inc. are different entities; Cavalry SPV I, LLC is not Cavalry Portfolio Services, LLC. Confirm the license was valid on the dates they were collecting from you — a lapse in the middle is its own violation.

Go through every letter they sent you

Colorado requires a collection agency’s initial written communication to carry this exact statement:

FOR INFORMATION ABOUT THE COLORADO FAIR DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES ACT, SEE HTTPS://COAG.GOV/OFFICE-SECTIONS/CONSUMER-PROTECTION/CONSUMER-CREDIT-UNIT/COLLECTION-AGENCY-REGULATION/

Missing, or pointing at a dead URL? That’s a CFDCPA violation sitting in your file drawer.

CFDCPA remedies — C.R.S. § 5-16-113: actual damages plus additional damages up to $1,000, with a one-year limitations period. You can’t recover under both the CFDCPA and the federal FDCPA for the same conduct, so pick the better vehicle — the federal act’s attorney-fee provision is usually stronger.

Leverage the federal route doesn’t give you

File a complaint with the Colorado AG’s Consumer Credit Unit. They don’t just receive complaints — they hold the license. A regulator that can suspend a debt buyer’s ability to operate in Colorado gets a very different quality of attention than a CFPB complaint does.

Discovery works differently here — adjust the plan

The discovery section below assumes district-court practice. County Court is not that. Under C.R.C.P. 316, discovery is available only by order of the court, requested at the pretrial conference. You cannot simply serve requests for production.

  1. Appear on the appearance date and ask for a pretrial conference.
  2. Request leave to conduct discovery, narrowly. Judges grant targeted requests far more readily than fishing expeditions. Name four things: the complete cardholder agreement and change-in-terms notices in effect at default; the full chain of assignment specifically identifying this account number; all monthly statements from opening to charge-off; the account-level data file behind the bill of sale.
  3. Subpoena duces tecum for trial if discovery is denied — you can still compel documents and witnesses to show up.
  4. Lean harder on trial objections. The plaintiff still has to prove its case with admissible evidence. In a limited-discovery forum, the hearsay-and-foundation attack on the robo-affidavit carries proportionally more of the weight.

Arbitration, Colorado edition

Cite both authorities in the motion: 9 U.S.C. §§ 2, 3, 4 and C.R.S. § 13-22-207, the Colorado Revised Uniform Arbitration Act’s motion-to-compel provision.

§ 13-22-207 is friendlier than the federal statute in one respect worth quoting: it directs the court to stay the judicial proceeding while the motion to compel is pending, and to stay it again once arbitration is ordered. You get the stay in two places, not one. Combined with Smith v. Spizzirri on the federal side, ask for the stay expressly in your prayer for relief.

If it goes badly — Colorado protects you better than most

Worth knowing before you evaluate any settlement offer, because it sets your real downside.

Wages — C.R.S. § 13-54-104. A judgment creditor takes the lesser of 20% of disposable earnings per pay period (federal law allows 25% — Colorado is tighter), or the amount by which weekly disposable earnings exceed 40× the Colorado minimum wage. At the 2026 state wage of roughly $15.16/hr, about $606/week of disposable earnings is fully exempt. Verify the current figure — the state wage is indexed annually, and while Boulder County and the City of Boulder set higher local wages, the garnishment formula runs off the state number.

Bank accounts — Colorado exempts roughly $2,500 cumulatively across depository accounts in your name. Confirm the current amount in C.R.S. § 13-54-102; the legislature has adjusted it recently.

Trap — exemptions are not automatic

After a garnishment notice you have roughly 10 days to file a written claim of exemption with the court. Miss it and the money goes, exemption or not. You can also ask the court at a hearing to exempt a greater share of your earnings if you need it to support yourself or your family.

01

The first 72 hours

Before any strategy. This part decides more cases than every legal theory combined.

  1. Find your answer deadline on the summons. Typically 14–30 days from service; justice / small-claims deadlines run shorter. Calendar it with a five-day-early alarm.
  2. Record how and when you were served — in person, left with a housemate, by mail. Defective service is its own motion.
  3. Pull the docket from the county clerk. Get the complaint and every exhibit they filed.
  4. Order your three credit reports at annualcreditreport.com and check the tradeline dates against what they’re alleging.
Never — these three do the most damage

Don’t make a payment. In most states a partial payment or written acknowledgment restarts the statute of limitations on a time-barred debt. A $20 “good faith” payment can revive a dead claim.

Don’t ignore it. Default judgments support wage garnishment, bank levy, and property liens, and they’re renewable for years.

Don’t give them ACH access or a debit card number. Ever, at any stage, including settlement.

02

File the answer regardless

Even if arbitration is your plan. A pending motion doesn’t reliably toll the answer deadline.

The answer is also where you preserve arbitration so they can’t argue you gave it up.

Deny

Deny what you can honestly deny. For anything else: “Defendant lacks sufficient knowledge or information to form a belief as to the truth of the allegation and therefore denies it.” Demand strict proof of every element. Many states permit a general denial — check your rules.

Plead your affirmative defenses or lose them

  • Lack of standing — plaintiff has not proven ownership of this account
  • Statute of limitations
  • Failure to state a claim upon which relief can be granted
  • Lack of privity — no contract between plaintiff and defendant
  • Failure to attach documents supporting the claim
  • “This dispute is subject to a binding arbitration agreement. Defendant does not waive and expressly reserves the right to compel arbitration.”
Never admit

That the account is yours. The balance. The date of default. That the plaintiff owns it. Not in the answer, not on the phone, not in an email.

03

The arbitration play

Four gates. Clear all four and it works. Fail any one and it’s dead.

Gate 1Does a clause actually exist?

Usually yes: Synchrony Bank (the largest single source of debt-buyer paper — Amazon, PayPal Credit, Lowe’s, CareCredit, most retail cards), Comenity/Bread, Citi, American Express, Discover, Credit One, First Premier, most personal-loan and auto paper.

Often no: Capital One dropped arbitration from most consumer card agreements after the 2010 antitrust settlement and has largely kept it out. Chase and Bank of America removed theirs around 2009–2010. If your account originated at Capital One, there may be nothing to compel.

Get the actual agreement — the version in effect at your date of default:

  • The CFPB Credit Card Agreement Database, which collects issuers’ agreements quarterly
  • A Request for Production: “the complete cardholder agreement, including all change-in-terms notices, in effect on [date of default]”
  • Their own exhibits — they often attach it themselves to a summary judgment motion
Gate 2Can it be enforced in this case?

Assignment. Read the definitions section. Most clauses extend to “successors and assigns,” putting the debt buyer in the bank’s shoes. Some courts have held an assignee falls outside the parties who may compel.

Dead-forum problem. Older agreements name the National Arbitration Forum as the exclusive forum. NAF left consumer debt arbitration in 2009 under a Minnesota AG consent decree. Courts split on whether that voids the clause or whether a substitute arbitrator is appointed under FAA § 5. Know your jurisdiction before relying on it.

Delegation clause. Many clauses send arbitrability itself to the arbitrator. That usually helps you — it shrinks what the judge gets to decide.

Gate 3The small-claims carve-out

This is the most common reason the play fails, and the video never mentions it.

Public Citizen’s survey of large card issuers found all but two carve small claims court out of their arbitration clauses. Some clauses exempt “collection actions” outright. Debt buyers know this and file in justice / small-claims court specifically to stay outside the clause.

Before you spend a filing fee: find the exception in the clause and check whether the court named on your summons falls inside it.

Gate 4Have you already waived it?

A party opposing arbitration no longer has to show it was prejudiced to prove the other side waived the right. Ordinary waiver rules apply: act inconsistently with the right to arbitrate and you lose it.

Morgan v. Sundance, 596 U.S. 411 (2022) — unanimous

Translation: move early. Assert it in the answer, file the motion promptly. Don’t run months of merits discovery, don’t file a counterclaim first, don’t fight a summary judgment motion and then pivot. Post-Sundance that reads as waiver.

The filing itself

Motion to Compel Arbitration and to Stay Proceedings, citing 9 U.S.C. §§ 2, 3, 4 plus your state’s arbitration act. Attach:

  1. The cardholder agreement, arbitration provision highlighted
  2. Your short declaration: you opened the account with [original creditor], the attached agreement governs, you did not opt out, and you elect arbitration
  3. The assignment language binding the plaintiff as assignee
  4. Argument that the claim is within the clause’s scope — they’re drafted extremely broadly, which cuts your way here
Say the magic words

Request a stay, not a dismissal, in your prayer for relief. Under Smith v. Spizzirri, 601 U.S. 472 (2024), when a court finds a dispute arbitrable and a party requests a stay, § 3 requires the court to stay — it has no discretion to dismiss. A stay parks the case and keeps the burden on the plaintiff. A dismissal without prejudice just lets them refile or resell the paper.

Why they fold — run their math

  • AAA Consumer Rules cap the consumer’s filing fee near $225. The business pays a four-figure filing fee, a case management fee, a final fee, and arbitrator compensation typically $1,500–$2,500+ for documents-only, more for a hearing. All-in exposure runs $3,000–$5,000+ on one account. (Fee schedules change — verify the current schedule at adr.org.)
  • JAMS Consumer Minimum Standards: when the consumer initiates, the consumer’s only required fee is $250 and the company pays the rest. When the company initiates against a consumer, the company pays all costs.
  • The AAA’s 2009 moratorium is the kill shot. The AAA stopped administering consumer debt collection arbitrations where the company is the filing party and the consumer had not agreed to arbitrate at the time of the dispute — and never lifted it. Practical effect: after arbitration is compelled, the debt buyer often cannot file its collection claim at the AAA at all.

They bought your $4,000 account for maybe $40–$120 inside a portfolio. Their model is volume and default judgments at roughly fifty dollars of attorney time per case. Forcing them into a forum that costs thousands per account — if it will even take the case — destroys the unit economics. So they dismiss. That is the play, not some magic sentence.

After you win the motion — where people lose a case they already won

Trap

Nothing happens automatically. The court stays the case and the file goes quiet.

The plaintiff bears the burden of prosecuting its own claim. You generally don’t need to initiate arbitration on a debt you dispute — they do. Don’t volunteer to file and pay a fee to prosecute their case for them.

But read the order. Some judges order the movant to initiate within a set number of days. If yours says that, comply or the stay gets lifted and they take their judgment.

Calendar a docket check every 30–60 days. Watch for a motion to lift the stay for failure to prosecute. Never let one go unopposed.

If you want affirmative relief — an FDCPA claim, statutory damages — then you initiate, for ~$225 (AAA) or $250 (JAMS), with the other side paying the freight.

Eventually the stayed case dies for want of prosecution. Push for dismissal WITH prejudice. Without prejudice, they can refile or resell.

04

The defenses that win more often than arbitration

Arbitration is a procedural detour. This is where defended debt-buyer cases actually collapse. Run these in parallel.

Standing and chain of title

They must prove they own your specific account, not a portfolio. Their standard proof is a generic Bill of Sale referencing “a pool of accounts” plus a spreadsheet excerpt. Demand the unbroken chain — original creditor → every intermediate buyer → plaintiff — with a document at each link that identifies your account number, name, and balance. One generic or missing link is fatal. The burden is theirs; you never have to prove they don’t own it.

The robo-affidavit

Their case rests on an affidavit from an employee who never worked at the original bank. Attack the foundation: no personal knowledge of how the originating creditor created or maintained those records; importing another company’s data into your database doesn’t make it your business record. Object to the statements as hearsay absent a qualified custodian from the originating bank. Courts split — some allow an integrating business to lay foundation — but in state collection court this objection wins a lot.

Statute of limitations

Runs from date of first default in most states — not last payment, not charge-off. Roughly 3–6 years depending on the state. Watch for a choice-of-law clause importing the original creditor’s home-state limitations period, which can be shorter than yours. And again: never pay on a time-barred debt.

Discovery is the real weapon

Serve it early. Most debt buyers cannot answer this without producing documents they simply don’t have — and that is when the dismissal arrives.

  • Requests for Production: the complete cardholder agreement and all change-in-terms notices in effect at default; the full chain of assignment identifying this account; every monthly statement from opening through charge-off; the purchase agreement and the account-level data file; all communications with defendant.
  • Interrogatories: identify each person with personal knowledge; state the date of first default; state the price paid for this account; itemize principal, interest, and fees separately.
  • Requests for Admission: admit you hold no agreement bearing defendant’s signature; admit you did not originate this account; admit you have no witness with personal knowledge of the original creditor’s record-keeping.
Trap — cuts both ways

Requests for admission that go unanswered are deemed admitted. The moment theirs arrive, calendar the response date. This is how well-defended cases get lost.

05

The counter-offensive

Their conduct around the lawsuit often creates claims worth more than the debt.

FDCPA — 15 U.S.C. § 1692e (false or misleading representations), § 1692f (unfair practices), § 1692i (wrong venue). Classic triggers: suing on a time-barred debt, suing without the right to sue, misstating the amount, affidavits from people with no knowledge. $1,000 statutory damages, plus actual damages, plus attorney’s fees and costs. One-year limitations period from the violation.

The actual leverage

It isn’t the $1,000 — it’s the fee-shifting. A consumer attorney will take an FDCPA case on contingency because the collector pays their fees, and will often defend the collection suit for free as part of it. That single fact reverses who is under cost pressure.

FCRA — § 1681s-2(b) if they re-aged the debt, reported it inaccurately, or failed to investigate. You must dispute through the credit bureaus in writing to trigger the furnisher’s duty; disputing only with the collector doesn’t.

State law — most states have a mini-FDCPA or UDAP statute with better damages and a longer limitations period than the federal act. Check yours first.

Sequencing

A counterclaim is litigation conduct. If you intend to compel arbitration, move to compel first, or bring the counterclaim inside the arbitration. Filing it first hands them a Sundance waiver argument.

06

If you settle

Negotiate from what they actually paid, and get every term in writing before a dollar moves.

They bought the account at roughly 2–8 cents on the dollar. Everything above that is profit. Openings of 15–30% of the balance are routinely accepted — lower once a motion to compel arbitration or a real discovery demand is on the docket.

Non-negotiable terms in the written agreement

  • Dismissal with prejudice
  • Deletion of the tradeline from all three bureaus — negotiable, but only if it’s in the document
  • No sale or transfer of any remaining balance to anyone
  • Whether they will issue a Form 1099-C for forgiven amounts over $600 — a “win” can create a tax bill
  • Payment by cashier’s check or money order only
Never

Accept a confession of judgment or a stipulated judgment that gets entered if you miss a payment. That is a default judgment with extra steps and a signature.

07

Checklist

Pull these before you write anything. Calendar these before you file anything.

Documents

  • Summons and complaint with every exhibit
  • Proof, date, and method of service
  • Cardholder agreement in effect at default (CFPB database or discovery)
  • All collection letters and the § 1692g validation notice
  • Three credit reports — check the tradeline dates
  • Bank records showing date of last payment
  • A call log: date, time, name, what was said

Deadlines

  • Answer due date minus five days
  • Motion to compel arbitration — as early as possible
  • Your discovery served / their responses due
  • Your responses to their requests for admission
  • 30- and 60-day stay check-ins after arbitration is compelled
  • FDCPA one-year limitations date

Where to get help

  • NACA — consumeradvocates.org. Consumer attorney directory; many take FDCPA cases on contingency and defend the collection suit as part of it.
  • Your state or county legal aid office and the court’s self-help center.
  • CFPB complaint portal — consumerfinance.gov/complaint. Creates a paper trail and often produces a substantive response.
