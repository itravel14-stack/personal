# Sued by a Debt Buyer — Defense Playbook

Midland (MCM/Midland Funding), Cavalry SPV, Velocity Investments, Portfolio Recovery,
LVNV/Resurgent, Jefferson Capital, Absolute Resolutions.

**Not legal advice.** Deadlines, forms, and procedure vary by state and by court level
(justice/small claims vs. district/circuit). Verify everything against your own summons
and your state's rules of civil procedure before filing anything.

---

## 0. Bottom line up front

The video is **directionally right and materially wrong**.

| Claim in the video | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Arbitration kills 90% of their lawsuits" | No data supports 90%. It works *when the gates below are cleared*. It's one tool, not a master key. |
| "The moment you say 'I elect arbitration,' the judge must stop the case" | False. Saying it does nothing. You must **file a written Motion to Compel Arbitration and Stay Proceedings**, attach the agreement, and prove the clause applies. |
| "It triggers the FAA" | True — 9 U.S.C. §§ 2, 3, 4 — but only if a valid, applicable clause exists *and* you haven't waived it. |
| "They lose their power instantly" | Their **economics** collapse, not their rights. That's still the real leverage. |

**The single highest-value action is not arbitration.** More than 70% of debt collection
suits end in default judgment because the defendant never files anything. Fewer than 10% of
defendants have a lawyer. Filing a timely Answer moves you out of the bucket where they win
automatically. Do that first, always, even if you plan to arbitrate.

---

## 1. First 72 hours

1. **Find the answer deadline.** It's on the summons. Typically 14–30 days from service;
   justice-court/small-claims deadlines can be shorter. Put it in your calendar with a
   5-day-early alarm. Missing it = default judgment = wage garnishment, bank levy, property lien.
2. **Note how and when you were served.** Personal service? Left with a housemate? Mail?
   Defective service is its own motion to dismiss.
3. **Pull the docket** (county clerk website or in person). Get the complaint and every exhibit.
4. **Do not call them.** Do not pay anything, not even $5.
5. **Do not admit the debt** in writing, by phone, or in your Answer.

### The three actions that will hurt you most

- **Making a payment.** In many states a partial payment or written acknowledgment
  **restarts the statute of limitations** on a time-barred debt. This is the most common
  self-inflicted wound in the entire space.
- **Ignoring it.** Default judgments are enforceable for years and are renewable.
- **Giving them your bank account or a post-dated ACH authorization.** Never.

---

## 2. The Answer (never skip this)

File it before the deadline even if your plan is arbitration. Two reasons: a pending
motion doesn't always toll the answer deadline, and an Answer is where you **preserve**
arbitration so they can't argue you waived it.

**Structure:**

- **Deny everything you can honestly deny**, or state you "lack sufficient knowledge or
  information to form a belief as to the truth of the allegation and therefore deny it."
  A general denial is permitted in many states — check your rules.
- **Demand strict proof** of every element.
- **Affirmative defenses** (plead them or you lose them):
  - Lack of standing / failure to prove ownership of the account
  - Statute of limitations
  - Failure to state a claim
  - Lack of privity / no contract between plaintiff and defendant
  - Failure to attach the account stated / documents supporting the claim
  - **The dispute is subject to a binding arbitration agreement; defendant does not
    waive and expressly reserves the right to compel arbitration**
  - Accord and satisfaction, payment, unclean hands (only if factually true)

**Never admit in the Answer:** that the account is yours, the balance, the date of default,
or that the plaintiff owns it.

Serve a copy on plaintiff's counsel and keep proof of service.

## 3. The arbitration play

### Gate 1 — Does an arbitration clause exist?

This is the whole ballgame. No clause, no play.

- **Usually yes:** Synchrony Bank (the biggest single source of debt-buyer paper — Amazon,
  PayPal Credit, Lowe's, Care Credit, most retail cards), Comenity/Bread, Citi, American
  Express, Discover, Credit One, First Premier, most auto and personal-loan paper.
- **Usually no:** **Capital One** dropped its arbitration clause from most consumer card
  agreements after the 2010 antitrust settlement and has largely kept it out.
  Chase and Bank of America removed theirs in 2009–2010 for cards. If your account is
  Capital One-originated, there may be nothing to compel.
- **Verify, don't assume.** Get the actual cardholder agreement in effect for your account:
  - **CFPB Credit Card Agreement Database** — the CFPB collects issuers' agreements
    quarterly. Search by issuer and pull the version covering your account's date.
  - **Request for Production** to the plaintiff: "the complete cardholder agreement,
    including all change-in-terms notices, in effect on [date of default]."
  - The plaintiff's own exhibits — they often attach the agreement to their complaint or
    to a motion for summary judgment, which hands it to you.

### Gate 2 — Can it be enforced here?

- **Assignment.** Most clauses expressly extend to "successors and assigns," so the debt
  buyer steps into the bank's shoes — which means *you* can enforce it against them too.
  But not always: some courts have held an assignee outside the defined parties who may
  compel. Read the definitions section of the clause.
- **Dead forum problem.** Older agreements name the **National Arbitration Forum** as the
  exclusive forum. NAF exited consumer debt arbitration in 2009 under a Minnesota AG
  consent decree. Courts split on whether an NAF-exclusive clause is void (forum is
  "integral") or whether a substitute arbitrator gets appointed under FAA § 5. Know which
  way your circuit/state goes before you rely on it.
- **Delegation clause.** Many clauses delegate arbitrability itself to the arbitrator.
  That usually helps you — it shrinks what the judge decides.

### Gate 3 — The small-claims carve-out (the most common killer)

**Nearly every major issuer's arbitration clause exempts small claims court.** Public
Citizen's survey found all but two large card issuers carve it out. Debt buyers know this
and deliberately file in justice/small-claims court **specifically to stay under the
arbitration clause**. Some clauses also carve out "collection actions" outright.

Before you spend a filing fee: read the clause, find the exception, and check whether the
court you were sued in falls inside it. If it does, arbitration is off the table — go
straight to Section 4.

### Gate 4 — Have you waived it?

**Morgan v. Sundance, 596 U.S. 411 (2022)** — the Supreme Court held unanimously that a
party opposing arbitration no longer has to show *prejudice* to prove waiver. Ordinary
waiver rules apply: act inconsistently with the right to arbitrate and you lose it.

Practical translation: **move early.** Assert it in your Answer, file the motion promptly.
Don't conduct months of merits discovery, don't file a counterclaim first, don't litigate
a summary-judgment motion and then pivot to arbitration. Post-*Sundance*, that's waiver.

### The filing

**Motion to Compel Arbitration and to Stay Proceedings**, citing **9 U.S.C. §§ 2, 3, 4**
and your state's arbitration act (most states have a UAA/RUAA analog).

Include:
1. A copy of the cardholder agreement with the arbitration provision highlighted.
2. Your short declaration/affidavit: you opened the account with [original creditor], the
   attached agreement governs, you have not opted out, and you elect arbitration.
3. The assignment language showing the clause binds the plaintiff as assignee.
4. Argument that the claim falls within the clause's scope (they're usually written
   extremely broadly — "any claim, dispute or controversy arising from or relating to
   the account").
5. **Explicitly request a stay, not dismissal.**

> **Smith v. Spizzirri, 601 U.S. 472 (2024):** when a court finds a dispute arbitrable and
> a party **requests a stay**, FAA § 3 requires the court to stay — it has no discretion to
> dismiss. Ask for the stay in your prayer for relief. A stay keeps the case parked and
> keeps the burden on the plaintiff to move it; a dismissal without prejudice just lets
> them refile or sell the paper.

Set it for hearing. Show up. Bring three copies.

### Why they fold — the actual economics

This is the mechanism, and it's real:

- **AAA Consumer Arbitration Rules** cap the *consumer's* filing fee at roughly **$225**.
  The **business** pays the rest — a filing fee in the low four figures, a case-management
  fee, a final fee, plus arbitrator compensation typically running **$1,500–$2,500+** for
  documents-only and considerably more for a hearing. All-in exposure to the business is
  commonly **$3,000–$5,000+**. (Fee schedules change — verify the current AAA Consumer Fee
  Schedule at adr.org before you cite numbers.)
- **JAMS Consumer Minimum Standards of Procedural Fairness**: when the *consumer* initiates,
  the consumer's only required fee is **$250** and the company pays everything else. When
  the **company** initiates against a consumer, **the company pays all costs**, full stop.
- **The AAA's 2009 moratorium is the kill shot.** In July 2009 the AAA stopped administering
  consumer *debt collection* arbitrations — cases where the **company is the filing party**
  and the consumer had not agreed to arbitrate at the time of the dispute — and never lifted
  it. Practical effect: after arbitration is compelled, the debt buyer often **cannot file
  its collection claim at the AAA at all.**

Now do their math. They bought your $4,000 account for maybe $40–$120 in a portfolio.
Their model is volume and default judgments at ~$50/case of attorney time. Being forced
into a forum where they owe thousands in fees on one account — if the forum will even take
it — destroys the unit economics. They dismiss. That's the play.

### After you win the motion — the trap

**This is where people lose a case they already won.**

- The court compels arbitration and stays the case. Nothing happens automatically.
- **The plaintiff has the burden of prosecuting its own claim.** You generally do not need
  to initiate arbitration to collect a debt you don't owe — *they* do. Don't volunteer to
  file and don't pay a filing fee to prosecute their case for them.
- **But read the order.** Some judges order the *movant* to initiate within a set number of
  days. If your order says that, comply or the stay gets lifted and they get their judgment.
- **Docket a calendar check every 30–60 days.** Watch for a motion to lift the stay for
  failure to prosecute arbitration. If one lands, respond — don't let it go unopposed.
- If you *want* affirmative relief (an FDCPA claim, statutory damages), then yes, you
  initiate — for ~$225 (AAA) or $250 (JAMS) with the other side paying the freight.
- Eventually the stayed case dies of want of prosecution, or they move to dismiss. **Push
  for dismissal WITH prejudice.** Without prejudice, they can refile or resell.

## 4. The defenses that win more cases than arbitration

Arbitration is a procedural detour. These are merits defenses, and they're where most
defended debt-buyer cases actually fall apart. Run them in parallel.

### Standing / chain of title
The plaintiff must prove it owns **your specific account**, not a portfolio. Typical proof
is a generic Bill of Sale referencing "a pool of accounts" plus a spreadsheet excerpt.
Demand the complete, unbroken chain: original creditor → each intermediate buyer → plaintiff,
with a document at every link that **identifies your account number by name and balance**.
One missing or generic link is fatal. Burden is on them — you don't have to prove they
*don't* own it.

### Admissibility / the robo-affidavit
Debt buyers prove their case with an affidavit from an employee who has never worked at the
original bank. Attack the foundation:
- No personal knowledge of how the original creditor created or maintained the records.
- Importing another company's data into your database doesn't make it *your* business record.
- Object to the account statements as **hearsay** without a qualified custodian from the
  originating bank.
Courts have gone both ways — some circuits allow an integrating business to lay foundation
for records it relies on — but in state collection court this objection wins a lot.

### Statute of limitations
Runs from the **date of first default**, not last payment or charge-off, in most states.
Ranges roughly 3–6 years. Some states apply the *original creditor's* home-state SOL via
a choice-of-law clause (borrowing statutes) — that can be shorter than yours. **Do not
make a payment on a time-barred debt**; it can revive it.

### Account stated / no signed contract
There is usually no signed agreement — just a cardholder agreement plus alleged use. Make
them plead and prove the theory they're actually on (breach of contract vs. account stated
vs. unjust enrichment), and hold them to its elements.

### Wrong party / identity
Common with similar names, juniors/seniors, and identity theft. If it isn't yours, say so
clearly and early, and get a police report / FTC identity theft report on file.

### Discovery is the real weapon
Serve it early. Most debt buyers cannot answer this without producing documents they don't
have — and that's when they dismiss:
- **RFP:** complete cardholder agreement + all change-in-terms notices in effect at default;
  full chain of assignment documents identifying this account; all monthly statements from
  account opening to charge-off; the purchase agreement and the specific account-level data
  file; all communications with defendant.
- **Interrogatories:** identify each person with personal knowledge; state the date of first
  default; state the price paid for this account; itemize principal, interest, and fees.
- **Requests for Admission:** admit you have no signed agreement bearing defendant's
  signature; admit you did not originate this account; admit you have no witness with
  personal knowledge of the original creditor's record-keeping. *(RFAs are dangerous both
  ways — unanswered ones are deemed admitted. Calendar theirs immediately.)*

## 5. The counter-offensive

Their conduct in and around the lawsuit often creates claims worth more than the debt.

- **FDCPA, 15 U.S.C. § 1692** — § 1692e (false/misleading representations), § 1692f
  (unfair practices). Classic triggers: suing on a time-barred debt, suing without the
  right to sue, misstating the amount, filing in the wrong venue (§ 1692i), affidavits from
  people with no knowledge. **$1,000 statutory damages + actual damages + attorney's fees
  and costs.** One-year statute of limitations from the violation.
- **The fee-shifting is the leverage**, not the $1,000. A consumer lawyer will take an
  FDCPA case on contingency because the defendant pays their fees. That changes who is
  under cost pressure.
- **FCRA, 15 U.S.C. § 1681s-2(b)** — if they re-aged the debt, reported it inaccurately, or
  failed to investigate after you disputed through the bureaus. Dispute **through the
  credit bureaus in writing** to trigger the duty; disputing only with the furnisher doesn't.
- **State analogs** — most states have a mini-FDCPA or UDAP statute with better damages and
  a longer SOL than the federal act. Check yours.
- **Sequencing matters.** Filing a counterclaim is litigation conduct. If you intend to
  compel arbitration, move to compel **first**, or assert the counterclaim in the
  arbitration. Don't hand them a *Sundance* waiver argument.

## 6. Settlement math

If you decide to settle, negotiate from the actual numbers:

- They bought the account for roughly **2–8 cents on the dollar** in a portfolio.
- Anything above that is profit. Openings of 15–30% of the balance are routinely accepted,
  lower once a motion to compel arbitration or a discovery demand is on file.
- **Get all of this in a signed writing before a dollar moves:**
  - Dismissal **with prejudice**
  - "Paid in full" or, better, **deletion** of the tradeline from all three bureaus
    (deletion is negotiable; get it in the agreement or don't count on it)
  - No sale or transfer of any remaining balance
  - A clear statement of whether they will issue a **Form 1099-C** for forgiven amounts
    over $600 — a "deal" can create a tax bill
  - Payment by cashier's check or money order. **Never give ACH access or a debit card.**
- Never accept a **confession of judgment** or a stipulated judgment that gets entered if
  you miss a payment. That's a default judgment with extra steps.

## 8. Checklist

**Documents to gather**
- [ ] Summons and complaint + every exhibit
- [ ] Proof/date/method of service
- [ ] Cardholder agreement in effect at default (CFPB database or discovery)
- [ ] All collection letters and the § 1692g validation notice
- [ ] Your three credit reports (annualcreditreport.com) — check the tradeline dates
- [ ] Any prior payment records / bank statements showing date of last payment
- [ ] Notes on every phone call: date, time, name, what was said

**Deadlines to calendar**
- [ ] Answer due date — **minus 5 days**
- [ ] Motion to compel arbitration filed (as early as possible)
- [ ] Discovery served / their responses due
- [ ] Your responses to their Requests for Admission — *unanswered RFAs are deemed admitted*
- [ ] 30/60-day stay check-ins after arbitration is compelled
- [ ] FDCPA one-year SOL from the violation date

**Getting help**
- **NACA** (consumeradvocates.org) — consumer attorney directory; many take FDCPA cases
  on contingency and defend the collection suit for free as part of it.
- Your state/county **legal aid** and any court self-help center.
- **CFPB complaint portal** (consumerfinance.gov/complaint) — creates a paper trail and
  frequently produces a substantive response.
